Ichimoku Trend-Follower vs the Current Bot

Research · SOL · ZEC · NEAR · ADA · GALA · 2020 → 27 Oct 2026 · Binance 4-hour candles

Can a trend-following strategy that rides big swings do better than the current bot? This report puts a cloud-breakout trend-follower next to the current bot on the same five coins and the same costs: every year, every month, the number of winning and losing trades, how it holds up under stress, and an independent re-check by a second model (Opus) that rebuilt the test from scratch.

Ichimoku made more R (+658 vs +493), but it is not a better bot. Its profit rests on a handful of giant trends: the 10 best trades are 74% of all its profit, and without the 20 best trades (of 760) almost nothing is left. The current bot earns less but steadily: more winning trades, fewer losing months, no single trade that matters.
Verdict: run it, if at all, as a separate paper-traded sub-account beside the current bot, never as a replacement. Judge it after 6–12 months, because a few trades can decide the outcome either way.

How these numbers were made

  • Current bot: the live Lucid’io rules, unchanged (rules are confidential and not described here). Its engine reproduces the live bot trade for trade.
  • Ichimoku trend-follower: buys when a 4-hour candle closes above the Ichimoku cloud (sells when it closes below), only in the direction of the 200-candle average. First stop 2× the average candle range (ATR) away; then the stop trails 7× ATR behind the best price and only ever moves in the trade’s favour. No profit target: a winner runs until the trail catches it.
  • Fair picking: the settings were chosen using 2020–2022 only, then frozen and judged on 2023–2026 and on coins that were never used (BTC, ETH and six more). Many settings were tried, so some luck in the pick is still possible.
  • Account figures: $1,000 start, 2.5% of current equity risked per trade, compounded, up to five coins open together, closed trades only. A trade counts in the month and year it closes. 2020 starts in Feb–Mar, 2026 ends in October.
  • Costs: 0.07% per side (fee + slippage) plus funding. Every decision uses only candles that had already closed (checked: cutting off recent data changes no earlier trade).
  • Live data: the live bot only started trading on 23 Oct 2026, so there are no real live trades to compare yet. “Current bot” here is its own engine on past prices.

74% of all Ichimoku profit comes from 10 trades out of 760. That is how trend-following works: lots of small losses, a few giant wins. It also means the result depends on catching the next huge trend. Without its single best trade (ZEC, Sep–Nov 2025) it is still up in all 7 years, but 2025 shrinks to +2R.

The current bot has no such trade: its biggest winners are only a few R, and its profit comes from hundreds of ordinary trades. That is the main reason it compounds more smoothly.

10 · Independent re-check (Opus)

A second model rebuilt the whole Ichimoku test from a plain-English description, without seeing the original code, then compared with it.

  • Exact match: same 760 trades, same entry times and directions, same +658R, same yearly numbers on every coin. Largest difference on any trade: 0.00000005R.
  • No look-ahead: the cloud uses only past candles, entries fill at the next candle’s open, stops are checked inside every candle (a gap through a stop fills at the worse price).
  • Data: no gaps in any file, no still-forming candle used.
  • Real funding instead of the flat charge would add about +8R, so the flat charge is slightly pessimistic.
  • Flagged as the real risks: (1) profit concentrated in a few trades; (2) it is a long-side strategy riding bull trends; (3) the coins and the 2×/7× setting were chosen from many tries, and the 2023–26 window has been looked at in several rounds, so it is no longer truly unseen. One hidden 600-candle time limit in the test code never triggered.

  • The two are barely related (monthly results correlate only 0.15), so together they have a smaller drop than the current bot alone, at the price of a somewhat smaller end balance.
  • 83% of Ichimoku trades overlap in time with a current-bot trade on the same coin; 86 of those run in opposite directions. In one account these would net off, and a trailing stop could move the stop of the other bot’s trade.
  • So use a separate sub-account for Ichimoku, with its own balance, stop orders and trade log.

12 · Bottom line

  • Ichimoku earns more R in total, with a similar worst drop and less leverage, and is up in all 7 years. Nothing in the tests (costs, delay, nearby settings, coins it never saw, independent rebuild) broke it.
  • It does not beat the current bot on steadiness: lower win rate (32% vs 39%), more losing months (37 vs 20), more losing coin-years, and 74% of profit from 10 trades. A broad search of ~3,000 settings across seven strategy families found nothing that beat the current bot on every measure.
  • The bot would need a trailing-stop feature before it could trade this strategy, because it currently places a fixed stop and target only.
  • Next step: paper-trade it in its own sub-account for 6–12 months and compare with this report.
