How your money works in the pool

A plain-language guide for friends who are thinking of joining. It follows four people through 2026 so you can see what joining, growing, the 10% fee and the bad weeks actually look like.

The example uses a backtest: the trading bot's rules replayed on real 2026 prices (Jan 1 to Oct 21). It shows how things work, not what will happen.

The short version

One account, one bot, everyone owns a slice

1 pool

All the money sits in one trading account. The manager's own money is in it too, trading the same way.

Units

Your money buys units. Your balance is your units × the unit price. The price moves only with the trading.

10%

The fee is 10% of new profit only. Nothing on the money you put in, no monthly or sign-up charge.

Can drop

The balance goes down as well as up. You can lose part or all of it. Nothing is guaranteed or insured.

Joining, step by step

What happens to your ₱1,000

Here is Friend A, who put in ₱1,000 on February 1.

  1. You send the money

    It lands in the trading account. It is recorded as received but does not own anything yet.

  2. It waits for the next weekly valuation

    Every Sunday night the manager records what the account is worth. Waiting money is left out of that number, so you never share in profit (or losses) from before you arrived.

  3. It buys units at that day's price

  4. Your units go up and down with the trading

    Everyone's money moves by the same % each week. Your share of the profit is simply your share of the units.

  5. Every quarter end, the 10% fee is charged

    Only on profit above your best-ever level. It is paid as a few of your units, inside the pool; no cash moves.

  6. After 3 months, you can take money out

    Ask in the app. You are paid within 7 business days at the value on the day of payout. For Friend A that unlock date was May 1.

The exchange and the bot

The money sits on a crypto exchange. The exchange can freeze, fail or be blocked, and the bot can stop or make mistakes. Any of these can cost money.

Not a bank, not registered

This is a private arrangement among friends, kept in a ledger by the manager. It is not a bank account, not insured and not a registered investment.

Your money is locked for 3 months

Each deposit can be withdrawn only 3 months after it joins, then within 7 business days. Only put in money you will not need soon.

Questions friends ask

Good to know

Does the manager pay the 10% too?

No. The manager's own money pays no fee, and it makes exactly the same trading % as yours over the same weeks. The app shows you both side by side.

If I join after a good month, do I get that profit?

No, and you do not pay for an earlier loss either. New money waits for the next valuation and buys in at that day's price.

Can I see my money?

Yes. You get your own login to the Lucid'io app: your balance after the fee, a growth chart, every entry, and what the fee has been. You cannot see other people's amounts, and they cannot see yours.

Can I add more later?

Yes. Each top-up is its own deposit with its own 3-month lock and its own best-ever line, so adding money never changes what an earlier deposit owes.

How much should I put in?

Only an amount you could lose without it hurting you. In the example, a ₱2,500 deposit was worth as little as at one point.

Backtest on real past market data. SOL and ZEC combined, Binance futures prices, from the first trade on Mar 12, 2020 (when the price history starts) to Oct 21, 2026: about 6 years and 7 months. Each trade risks 1% (or 5%) of the balance at that moment, so every win and loss compounds into the next trade. Trading fees, slippage and funding costs are included. Shown before the 10% fee, with no money added or taken out. The two coins were chosen after seeing these results, so real trading will very likely do worse.
Read the 5% numbers with care. A balance that falls by about two thirds before recovering is very hard to sit through, and most people would stop at the bottom. At large sizes real orders also move the price, so the later years at 5% could not be repeated in practice. The bot also limits how much it risks on one trade, which would slow the growth. The 1% line is the more realistic picture.
