The four-coin system at 5% risk

How it works, in plain words

A fixed set of rules run by a computer on four large, liquid coins. It never guesses and has no emotions.

1
Four coins, one rule

Solana, Zcash, NEAR and Cardano. On the 4-hour chart the system trades when price closes beyond its Bollinger Band in the direction of the 200-period trend, both long and short.

2
5% risked per trade

Every trade has its exit for a loss set before it opens, placed so the loss is 5% of the account (₱250 on ₱5,000). Wins are targeted at 4 times that risk.

3
Two ways to size

Fresh: every 1 January the account is reset to ₱5,000. Compounding: every 1 January the 5% is recalculated from the new, larger balance.

What to expect

  • Most trades lose. Only about 39% win. Wins average +2.3R and losses −0.95R, so the wins pay for everything.
  • The balance dips below the start in almost every first year. 63 of 69 possible starting months went below the starting balance at some point, by about ₱1,256 on average.
  • It works in both directions. Longs and shorts both made money; shorts carried the 2022 bear market.
  • The edge is slowly shrinking. Profit factor went from 1.94 in 2021 to 1.36 in 2026.

Fresh ₱5,000 each year

You start every January with exactly ₱5,000 and risk a flat ₱250 per trade all year. Profit is paid out or set aside at year end.

  • Total profit +₱102,197
  • Average year +₱14,600 (+292%)

Compounding year to year

Start with ₱5,000 and keep everything in. On each 1 January the 5% is recalculated, so risk per trade grows from ₱250 to ₱954,520.

  • Final balance ₱56,130,897 (×11226.2)
  • Biggest single-year gain: +₱37,040,496
  • Worst drop in pesos: ₱17,419,263 (2026)
  • Percent drops are the same as fresh, but each one costs far more pesos as the account grows
Treat the compounding figure as a ceiling, not a forecast. It assumes the 2020–2026 pattern repeats, the coins stay liquid at ever bigger size, and nothing is withdrawn. A ₱56,130,897 result would need position sizes the order books of these coins cannot fill without moving price. What this means

  • Starting negative is normal. About one start in three (25 of 69) is below the starting balance after the first month, and nearly every start dips at some point in the first year.
  • It does not stay that way. By six months only 1 of 64 starts were still underwater, and no start finished its first twelve months in the red.
  • July and March were the roughest starting months. July is the only month with a negative average first month and the deepest average dip. March had the most negative first months (4 of 6). With six tries each, this is a pattern to be aware of, not a rule.
  • October, May and January were the gentlest.
  • The real risk is the size of the dip, not whether it happens. At 5% risk the rough starts lost 72% to 91% of the account before recovering. Only start with money you can leave alone through that.

How to choose

  • 5% is the aggressive setting. It roughly doubles profit, but the account has fallen by more than 50% from a high within a single year, and some starting months lost over 91% before recovering.

  • Pick the setting by the largest loss you could live with, not by the best-looking profit column.

How this was tested, and what it cannot tell you

Method

  • Data: real 4-hour candles for SOL, ZEC, NEAR and ADA from Binance futures, March 2020 to 2026-10-20.
  • Rules (never changed during the test): a 4-hour candle closes beyond the Bollinger Band (20, 2) in the direction of the 200-candle average; the stop is the middle band; the target is 4 times the stop distance; any trade still open after 48 candles (8 days) is closed. Entry on the next candle's open. Long and short.
  • Costs included: exchange fees (0.05% entry and stop, 0.02% target), slippage (0.02%) and the real funding rate paid on every open trade.
  • One trade at a time per coin; four coins can be open together, so up to 20% of the account is at risk at once at 5%.
  • Sizing: “Fresh” risks a flat 5% of ₱5,000 (₱250) on every trade of the year. “Compounding” risks 5% of the balance on each 1 January.
  • Not included: the 20% performance fee, tax, deposit and withdrawal costs, and anything that happens between the 4-hour candles.

What it cannot tell you

  • The four coins were chosen after seeing results. That makes the test look better than live trading is likely to be. The edge has been shrinking since 2021.
  • Real fills can be worse in fast markets, especially on bigger positions. The compounding numbers get less realistic the larger the account.
  • Six and a half years is one market cycle. A longer sideways or very different market is not in this data.
  • Calendar and weekday patterns are not reliable. Tests show they are consistent with chance.
  • Backtests are simulations, not a record of real trades.
Past results do not guarantee future returns. Only use money you can afford to lose. The test shows a system that made money in every year it was tested, but also one that regularly falls 20% to 50% below its high before recovering.

How the test works. For each year we find the worst stretch of trades, the run from the account's highest point down to its lowest point that year. What this tells you

  • The end result does not change, the ride does. Compounding multiplies each trade's result, and multiplication has no order. What changes is how far the balance falls before it starts to recover.
  • Worst case at 5%: the account falls to ₱8,105 in 2023, a loss of 73% of ₱30,000, against ₱17,046 (43%) in the normal order.
  • Worst case at 2.5%: ₱16,180 in 2023 (−46%), so halving the risk cuts the worst-case loss from 73% to 46%.
  • Per-trade compounding protects the account from a wipe-out because each loss is 5% of what is left. Losses get smaller as the balance falls, which is why the worst case stays under about 80% instead of reaching 100%. The trade-off is that gains also compound more slowly while the balance is down.
  • Sizing detail: trades are sized one after another in the order they closed. In real trading two or three trades are often open at once and sized from the same balance, so a real run can fall slightly further than shown.
