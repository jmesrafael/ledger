How it works, in plain words

The system is a set of fixed rules run by a computer. It watches large, liquid markets around the clock and only acts when its conditions are met. No guessing and no emotions.

1
You join the pool

Your ₱1,000 buys a slice of one shared trading pool. When the pool grows or shrinks, your slice moves by the same percent as everyone else's.

2
The loss is limited before every trade

Every trade has an exit point for a loss, set before the trade is opened. Each trade can lose at most about 5% of the pool.

3
The 20% fee on new profit only

Every two months, 20% of new profit above your best level so far is the fee. A loss means no fee, and a fee is never charged twice on the same profit.

What to expect

  • Most trades are small losses. Only about 39% of trades win. The system makes money because the wins are much bigger than the losses.
  • Some months are red, sometimes several in a row. That is normal for this kind of system. Page 9 shows every bad stretch.
  • The swings are big. With 5% of the pool at risk per trade, an investor's balance once fell −44% from its high before recovering, and single months lost up to −28%.
  • It does best when markets move strongly (big rallies or big sell-offs) and slowly gives back a little when prices drift sideways.
  • All trading costs are included: exchange fees, slippage (getting a slightly worse price than planned) and other charges, using conservative assumptions. The 20% fee is on top and already taken out of every peso figure here.
How the compounding works. We start with ₱1,000 on 1 February 2020. During each year, every trade puts 5% of the balance the pool had on 1 January of that year at risk. On 1 January of the next year, the size is recalculated from the new, bigger balance. So profit is re-invested once a year, and every year starts from the previous year's final balance. The 20% fee is taken every two months (end of February, April, June, August, October and December) from profit above your best level so far.

Red months vs green months

The system makes money from strong, one-directional moves. We compared the market in the 27 red months with the 54 green months:

In short: red months are quiet, choppy months. Prices moved about half as much and went sideways more, so trades kept failing. Jul–Sep is usually the calmest stretch for these markets, which is why 10 of its 21 months were red. August was red 4 of 7 times. May never ended red. Calm months mostly cost a little, while big trending months make the year.
Treat this as a tendency, not a rule. Each calendar month appears only 6–7 times, so a single year can change the picture. The weak quarter moves around from year to year. We do not switch the system off in any season, because some of the best months fell inside the "weak" quarter.

Important notes

Please read before drawing any conclusions from this report.

What this test is

  • A test on past prices: the trading rules were applied to historical prices to see what would have happened. It is a simulation, not a record of real trades.
  • Prices are real market data from a major exchange, February 2020 to 19 October 2026. Decisions are made only on information that was available at the time, and the test was checked to make sure it never "peeks" at future prices.
  • Costs are set on the conservative side: 0.08% exchange fee on every buy and sell, plus 0.05% slippage on every fill, plus the other charges of the period.
  • The 20% fee is taken every two months from new profit above your best level so far, as it would be in the real pool. Every peso figure in this report (except page 3) is after that fee.
  • Several markets are traded from one account. Their good and bad periods often fall at different times, which smooths the overall result.

What this test is not

  • Not a promise. Past results, simulated or real, do not guarantee future returns. Markets change, and a strategy that worked before can stop working.
  • Real trading can differ from the simulation: exchange outages, sudden price gaps, minimum order sizes and delays can make real results worse.
  • The rules were developed while looking at historical data, so some of the past performance may be better than what can be repeated.
  • 5% risk per trade is aggressive. It produces the large growth shown here, but also the deep drops on page 9. Lower risk means smaller swings and smaller returns.
  • The peso is not modelled. Results were computed in dollars and shown as ₱1,000 for easy reading. A stronger or weaker peso would change what your balance is worth in pesos.
  • These markets are very volatile. Only use money you can afford to lose.
