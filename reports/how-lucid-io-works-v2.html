1 · How it works

From your pocket to a bigger pool, in 6 steps

Think of a group savings pot that is traded by one person for everyone. The coloured labels are what you will see next to your money in the app.

1

You send your money Waiting

You hand the money to the fund manager. It is written down straight away, but it is not working yet: it can't gain and it can't lose.

2

We check that it arrived Waiting to join

Your money only counts once it has really reached the trading account at the exchange. Until then it stays outside the pool, so it can never be counted as profit by mistake.

3

You get your slices In the pool

Your money joins at that day's price and you receive units (think: slices of a pizza, see section 4). You never share in gains made before you joined, and you never pay for losses from before either.

4

The pool is traded Estimate

The fund manager trades the pool for everyone. You keep the same number of slices; what each slice is worth goes up and down. The balance you see in the app is an estimate until a settlement or a withdrawal makes it final.

5

Every two months, we settle Final

If your money is worth more than ever before, 20% of the new profit is the fee. If not, there is no fee. How the fee works.

6

You take your money out when you need it Paid

Ask in the app. You are paid within 7 business days, at the value on the day you are paid.

Why some investors are below zero, and why that is normal

  • In a few weeks, anything can happen. Prices can go down as easily as up. Someone who joins just before a dip starts in the red.
  • Time is what makes it work. The plan is built to grow slowly over many months: small wins add up and losses are kept small.
  • The investors who joined first are ahead.

So please see this as long-term money: plan to leave it in for at least a year and don't judge it by the first few weeks.

Long term helps, but it is not a guarantee. Only put in money you can afford to lose.

4 · Your slice

The pool is one big pizza

All the money sits together in one trading account at an exchange. Instead of tracking everyone's pesos, the ledger cuts the pool into equal slices. We call a slice a unit. You own some slices, other investors own some, and so does the fund manager.

An example pizza with made-up people. Tap the buttons to see what happens. The rule is always the same: the pizza gets bigger or smaller, and every slice grows or shrinks by the same percent. Nobody's slices change because someone else joins or leaves.

Why a request can wait a little

Up to two trades can be open at once. A payout is worked out only when both trades are closed and the balance is checked, so nobody is paid at the price of a half-finished trade. The app tells you the reason while you wait.

The 3-month lock-up

Your first deposit has a 3-month lock-up as a guideline: please plan to leave it in that long. If you need money earlier, ask; the fund manager decides, and any exception is written down.

Withdrawal fee: none for now. A small one may be introduced later, lower the longer your account has been open. You will be told before it starts.
6 · The fee

20% of new profit, never charged twice

There is only one fee, and you only pay it when you make new profit. Every investor has a best level: the highest your money has ever been worth. It starts at what you put in and rises with every deposit. Every two months we look at where you are:

Above your best level

20% of the part above it is the fee. Your best level then moves up to your new value.

Below your best level

No fee. A loss has to be made back first, before any fee is due again.

Money in or out

Deposits are never counted as profit and withdrawals are never counted as losses. They don't change the fee.

7 · Risks

Please read this part twice

Please remember what could happen

  • Never use money you need for rent, food, school, bills, emergencies, or money you borrowed. Only money you could lose without it hurting your life.
  • No guarantee. We work with statistics and probability, not certainty. Good past results do not mean the next trade, month or year will be good.
  • The market can change. Prices can move faster and further than ever before, or behave in ways the past never showed. The plan may then lose more than expected.
  • Losses can be large. You can lose a big part, or in the worst case all, of what you put in.
  • Be ready for red periods. There will be weeks and months where your balance is down. That is part of it; please don't put in money that would make you panic when that happens.

What "5% risk per trade" really means

Before every trade, the plan puts 5% of the current balance at risk (about ₱500 when the pool is worth ₱10,000). Wins make the next trade bigger, losses make it smaller. That is why results swing so much.

The deepest fall

In the test, the balance dropped 65% from a high point (May 2023). At 1% risk the worst fall was 14%. After a 65% fall you need about +185% just to get back to even.

Dips inside a single year

Even in years that ended well, the balance dipped between 28% and 57% from its high along the way (see the table in section 3).

Starting at a bad moment

Starting in any month and holding for 12 months (58 start dates), the typical result was about +260%, but 6 of 58 starts lost money, the worst by 31%. Starting at a peak, ₱1,000 was worth about ₱520 three months later (Oct 2022) and about ₱670 (Sep 26, 2026).

Recent years are weaker

In the last 12 months, 5% risk earned about +23% (1% earned about +13%). The extra risk bought much less than in 2021–2025, and the old totals are flattered because the markets were chosen after seeing how they did.

Source: tests on past prices up to Oct 21, 2026. A lower risk setting is calmer; 5% is what the pool uses, so expect red months and the occasional deep red stretch.

You can lose money

The market moves fast. The pool can fall sharply and you can lose a large part or all of what you put in. Only use money you can afford to lose.

The exchange and trading errors

The money sits at an exchange. An exchange problem, a hack or a trading error could cause losses that the plan did not expect.
