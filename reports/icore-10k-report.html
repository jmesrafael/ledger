Before any fee. Peso figures are the trading result with exchange fees, slippage and funding already taken out, and no performance fee. Risk per trade is ₱250 on a ₱10,000 balance (2.5%). I read your "2.5k" as 2.5% of the account; at ₱2,500 a trade (25%) the account would not survive its own drawdown.

Is it good? The short answer

  • The edge looks real but fragile. Average trade is +0.87R after costs (about 2.2% of the account per trade at 2.5% risk). The 95% range for that average is +0.43R to +1.41R, and positive in more than 99.9% of resamples.
  • It loses 68% of trades and relies on rare, huge winners. The 10 best trades are 74% of all profit. Without them the average falls to +0.23R.
  • Every calendar year was positive in this test, but 2.5% risk still saw drops of up to −53% from a high and a longest losing run of 16 trades.
  • Verdict: good enough to paper-trade and size small, not good enough to treat as proven. The settings were tuned on this same history.

Is 5% worth it? The growth-optimal fixed risk on this history is about 8.9% per trade, but only 5.1% once the 10 best trades are removed, and betting at or past half of that adds a lot of drawdown for little extra growth. With up to 5 coins open together, 5% each can put 25% of the account on the line at once. 5% is the aggressive end of what the history supports; 2.5% is the more defensible size, and it still saw a −53% drop.

How the numbers were made

  • Costs in every trade: 0.05% fee + 0.02% slippage each way, and 0.005% funding per 4 hours held, in both directions.
  • Size: a fixed % of the closed-trade balance at the moment of entry, one position per coin, up to five at once. Trades are credited to the year and month they close.

Limits of this test

  • Tuned on the same history. The settings were picked from many tries on 2020–2022, and later rounds also looked at 2023–2026. Treat all of this as a candidate to paper-trade.
  • Dependence on rare huge winners. The best single trade (ZEC in 2025) is about 128R, 19% of the whole result.
  • Coin choice. These five coins all had strong runs over this period; a weaker basket would likely do worse.
  • Real fills. Gaps, thin books and exchange order behaviour can be worse than the assumed costs, especially at compounded size.
  • Open trades. Positions still open at the end of the data (ZEC and ADA shorts) are not counted in the results above.
