How it works, in plain words

The system is a set of fixed rules run by a computer. It watches two large, liquid crypto markets around the clock and only acts when its conditions are met. No guessing and no emotions.

1
Wait for a strong move

It waits until price breaks out strongly. It only trades in the direction of the bigger trend, so it can profit when markets go up or down.

2
Limit the loss before entering

Every trade has an exit point for a loss, set before the trade is opened. Each trade can lose at most about 5% of the account.

3
Let winners run

The profit target is about 4 times the amount risked. If neither the loss limit nor the target is hit within 8 days, the trade is closed.

What to expect

  • Most trades are small losses. Only about 39% of trades win. The system makes money because the wins are much bigger than the losses.
  • Some months are red, sometimes several in a row. That is normal for this kind of system. Page 9 shows every bad stretch.
  • The swings are big. With 5% risked per trade, the account once fell −48% from its high before recovering, and single months lost up to −28%.
  • It does best when markets move strongly (big rallies or big sell-offs) and slowly gives back a little when prices drift sideways.
  • All trading costs are included: exchange fees, slippage (getting a slightly worse price than planned) and funding charges, using conservative assumptions.
How the compounding works. We start with $100 on 1 February 2020. During each year, every trade risks 5% of the balance the account had on 1 January of that year. On 1 January of the next year, the risk is recalculated from the new, bigger balance. So the profit is re-invested once a year, and every year starts from the previous year's final balance.

Why do the red months happen?

Each dot is one month of the test. Left to right = how much the two markets moved that month. Up/down = how the account did.

Red months vs green months

The system makes money from strong, one-directional moves. We compared the market in the 26 red months with the 54 green months:

Average over both marketsRed monthsGreen months
Price movement in the month18%37%
Trend score (how straight the move was)6.710.8
Trades that hit the full profit target6%22%
Trades that won23%47%
In short: red months are quiet, choppy months. Prices moved about half as much and went sideways more, so trades kept failing and few trades reached their profit target. Jul–Sep (summer) is usually the calmest stretch in crypto, which is why 10 of its 21 months were red. August was red 4 of 7 times. May never ended red. Calm months mostly cost a little, while big trending months make the year.
Treat this as a tendency, not a rule. Each calendar month appears only 6–7 times, so a single year can change the picture. Every quarter is still profitable on average, and the weak quarter moves around: Q3 and Q4 2022 were red, Q1 2023 was red, and early Q4 2026 is red. We do not switch the system off in any season, because some of the best months (e.g. July 2020 and August 2021) fell inside the "weak" quarter.

Important notes

Please read before drawing any conclusions from this report.

What this test is

  • A backtest: the trading rules were applied to historical prices to see what would have happened. It is a simulation, not a record of real trades.
  • Prices are real 4-hour market data from a major exchange, February 2020 to 19 October 2026. Decisions are made only on information that was available at the time, and the test was checked to make sure it never "peeks" at future prices.
  • Costs are set on the conservative side: 0.08% exchange fee on every buy and sell, plus 0.05% slippage on every fill, plus the real funding charges of the period.
  • Two markets are traded from one account. Their good and bad periods often fall at different times, which smooths the overall result.

What this test is not

  • Not a promise. Past results, simulated or real, do not guarantee future returns. Markets change, and a strategy that worked before can stop working.
  • Real trading can differ from the simulation: exchange outages, sudden price gaps, minimum order sizes and delays can make real results worse.
  • The rules were developed while looking at historical data, so some of the past performance may be better than what can be repeated.
  • 5% risk per trade is aggressive. It produces the large growth shown here, but also the deep drops on page 8. Lower risk means smaller swings and smaller returns.
  • Crypto is very volatile. Only use money you can afford to lose.
